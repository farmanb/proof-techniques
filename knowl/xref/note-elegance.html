Note 3.3.

A direct proof is usually considered more elegant than a proof by contradiction, and you should generally prefer one when it is available. A telltale sign that a proof by contradiction was unnecessary is when, after assuming \(A\) and \(\neg B\text{,}\) you derive \(\neg B\) and \(B\) themselves as your contradiction—that argument can usually be rewritten as a direct proof of \(A \implies B\text{.}\) On the other hand, if the contradiction you reach involves some \(Q\) genuinely different from \(B\text{,}\) a proof by contradiction was likely the right tool.
